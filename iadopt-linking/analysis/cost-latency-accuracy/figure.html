Accuracy, cost and latency of the three linkers

Bottom line. Accuracy cannot be ranked: the margins of error overlap. Jev and Qwen3.8-27B are about equally fast. Cost depends on where Qwen3.8-27B is bought: at a typical OpenRouter provider it would cost about 2.5× what Jev costs, and across providers it ranges from 4× cheaper than Jev to 12× dearer. The reranker has no per-call price but was more than twice as slow, on the Mac.

  • Same test for all. The same 284 slots, the same candidates and the same strict scoring (a Wikidata twin of a QUDT kind counts as wrong). The three accuracy ranges overlap, so they cannot be ranked.
  • One list is the 286 slots that have candidates, linked once. For Jev and Qwen3.8-27B the bar is the first candidate order (the pre-specified main run) and the line shows the other three orders; for the reranker it is one scoring run, with two earlier runs of the same job.
  • Latency adds up the time of every call, one at a time. Where it ran matters: the reranker on this Mac's GPU (including loading the model), Qwen3.8-27B on PSNC's server, Jev through OpenRouter. The Jev run sent 4 calls at a time, about 22 s of wall-clock time per list.
  • Cost. Jev is what OpenRouter billed. Qwen3.8-27B was free at PSNC, so it is priced as if bought on OpenRouter, from the 15 of 20 providers that offer log-probabilities (this method needs them). Prices were read on 2026-10-19 and they change. The reranker has no per-call price.
  • Post hoc. Put together after the results were known; nothing was re-run. Jev's yes/no formulation is not shown (177 of 284 correct).
